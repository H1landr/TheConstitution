Timeline

1777–2022 The making and remaking

The document's history in order. Amendments are dated to ratification, which is when they took effect, not when Congress proposed them.

Making the document

  • 1777The Articles of Confederation are adopted by Congress, after sixteen months of debate.
  • 1781The Articles take effect when Maryland becomes the last state to ratify — four years after adoption.
  • 1786Shays' Rebellion in Massachusetts exposes how little the national government can do.
  • 1787The Constitutional Convention meets in Philadelphia from May to September, in secret.
  • 1787September 17 — the Constitution is signed by 39 of the 55 delegates who attended.
  • 1787December 7 — Delaware becomes the first state to ratify.
  • 1788June 21 — New Hampshire becomes the ninth state, putting the Constitution into effect.
  • 1789March 4 — the first Congress convenes and the Constitution takes effect.
  • 1789April 30 — George Washington is inaugurated as the first president.
  • 1789September 25 — Congress proposes twelve amendments, including the one that becomes the Twenty-Seventh in 1992.
  • 1790May — Rhode Island becomes the last of the original thirteen to ratify, under economic pressure.

Establishing the structure

  • 1791December 15 — ten of the twelve proposed amendments are ratified. They become the Bill of Rights.
  • 1795February 7 — the Eleventh Amendment, the fastest in history, responds to a 1793 ruling that let citizens sue states.
  • 1803February 24 — Marbury v. Madison establishes judicial review.
  • 1804June 15 — the Twelfth Amendment separates the electoral ballots for president and vice president.
  • 1833Barron v. Baltimore holds that the Bill of Rights does not apply to the states.
  • 1857Dred Scott v. Sandford denies citizenship to Black Americans and is later overturned by the Fourteenth Amendment.

The second founding

  • 1861–65The Civil War.
  • 1861The Corwin Amendment, which would have protected slavery, passes Congress and is never ratified. It is still technically open.
  • 1865December 6 — the Thirteenth Amendment abolishes slavery.
  • 1868July 9 — the Fourteenth Amendment establishes birthright citizenship, due process, and equal protection against the states.
  • 1870February 3 — the Fifteenth Amendment bars racial discrimination in voting.
  • 1873The Slaughter-House Cases read the privileges or immunities clause almost out of existence.

Progressive era to Depression

  • 1895Pollock strikes down the federal income tax.
  • 1897The takings clause becomes the first provision applied to the states.
  • 1913February 3 — the Sixteenth Amendment permits an income tax without apportionment.
  • 1913April 8 — the Seventeenth Amendment provides for direct election of senators.
  • 1919January 16 — the Eighteenth Amendment begins Prohibition.
  • 1920August 18 — the Nineteenth Amendment extends the vote to women, after Tennessee ratifies by one vote.
  • 1933January 23 — the Twentieth Amendment moves inauguration to January 20 and ends the long lame-duck session.
  • 1933December 5 — the Twenty-First Amendment repeals Prohibition, the only amendment ratified by state conventions.

Postwar to present

  • 1951February 27 — the Twenty-Second Amendment limits presidents to two elected terms.
  • 1954Brown v. Board of Education holds that segregated schools violate equal protection.
  • 1961March 29 — the Twenty-Third Amendment gives Washington, D.C. three electoral votes.
  • 1963Gideon v. Wainwright requires states to provide lawyers to poor defendants in felony cases.
  • 1964January 23 — the Twenty-Fourth Amendment bans poll taxes in federal elections.
  • 1965The Voting Rights Act puts federal enforcement behind the Fifteenth Amendment.
  • 1966Miranda v. Arizona requires police warnings before custodial questioning.
  • 1967February 10 — the Twenty-Fifth Amendment sets out presidential succession and disability.
  • 1971July 1 — the Twenty-Sixth Amendment lowers the voting age to 18 in 100 days, the fastest ratification ever.
  • 1992May 7 — the Twenty-Seventh Amendment is ratified, 202 years after Congress proposed it.
  • 2008District of Columbia v. Heller recognizes an individual right to keep a firearm.
  • 2010McDonald v. Chicago applies that right to the states.
  • 2015Obergefell v. Hodges requires states to recognize marriages between two people of the same sex.
  • 2022Dobbs v. Jackson overturns Roe v. Wade; Bruen changes the test for gun regulations.

Note the shape of that last section. Between 1971 and 1992 there is one amendment. Between 1992 and today there are none. Almost everything that changed in the last fifty years changed through the courts.
